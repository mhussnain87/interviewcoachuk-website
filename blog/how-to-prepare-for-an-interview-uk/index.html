How to Prepare for an Interview: The Complete UK Guide (2026)

Updated 27 August 2026
Quick answer: Effective UK interview preparation follows six steps: understand the competencies being assessed, research the organisation, predict the likely questions, build STAR answers with evidence, practise out loud, and prepare your own questions. This guide walks through each.

Step 1: Understand what UK interviews actually assess

Most UK employers — the NHS, Civil Service, schools, police forces, and the majority of large private employers — run competency or values-based interviews. Panels score evidence of specific behaviours (communication, teamwork, problem-solving, leadership) against a framework, not general impressions. Knowing this changes how you prepare: your job is to bring scored evidence, not just to chat well. Our competency questions guide explains the format in depth.

Step 2: Research the role and organisation properly

Go beyond the About page. Read the job advert and person specification line by line — every listed duty and criterion is a potential question. Check recent news about the organisation, its values statement, and (for public sector roles) the relevant framework: NHS values, Civil Service Success Profiles behaviours, or the police Competency and Values Framework. Aim to connect at least one genuine reason you want this role at this organisation — the "why do you want to work here" question is near-universal.

Step 3: Predict the questions you'll be asked

Interview questions are more predictable than most candidates realise. Start with the most common UK interview questions, then layer in your sector's specifics from our guides below. The person specification is your cheat sheet: a criterion like "works effectively under pressure" almost guarantees a "tell me about a time you worked under pressure" question. The Interview Coach UK app automates this step — paste any job advert and it generates the likely questions for that exact role.

Step 4: Build STAR answers with real evidence

For each predicted question, prepare one concrete example structured as Situation, Task, Action, Result — the format UK panels are trained to score. Keep the situation brief, spend most of your answer on your actions (say "I", not "we"), and finish with a measurable result. Six to eight strong, flexible examples cover most interviews. Full walkthroughs: the STAR method explained and worked STAR examples.

Step 5: Practise out loud, not in your head

Answers that read well often fall apart spoken. Practise aloud — to a mirror, a friend, or a mock interview tool — and time yourself: two to three minutes per competency answer is the sweet spot. Record yourself once; it is uncomfortable and enormously useful. Structured practice with feedback is exactly what the app's mock interview and AI coach modes provide.

Step 6: Prepare your own questions

"Do you have any questions for us?" ends almost every UK interview, and "no" is the only wrong answer. Prepare three to five genuine questions about the role, team and development — our guide to questions to ask at the end of an interview has ready examples with the reasoning behind them.

Logistics and the day itself

Confirm the format (in person, video, panel size), plan the journey or test the tech the day before, re-read the job advert and your examples that morning, and arrive ten minutes early — UK surveys consistently find lateness among the fastest routes to rejection. Bring the energy to the first two minutes: first impressions are scored too, formally or not.

Prepare by sector

Generic preparation gets you to good; sector-specific preparation gets you to offer. Pick your field:

  • NHS, Healthcare & Social Care — NHS questions, Band 5, Band 6, Band 7, midwifery, HCA, paramedic, social work
  • Civil Service & Public Sector — Civil Service questions, behaviours, Success Profiles
  • Emergency Services — police officer questions
  • Education — teaching assistant, primary teaching
  • Business, Retail & Graduate — graduate schemes, retail management, Amazon
  • Core skills — tell me about yourself, strengths & weaknesses, behavioural questions

Key takeaways

  • UK interviews score evidence against competencies — prepare examples, not scripts
  • The job advert predicts most questions; six to eight STAR examples cover most interviews
  • Practise out loud with feedback — written prep alone is not enough
  • Always bring questions of your own

Frequently asked questions

How long should I spend preparing for an interview?

UK candidates commonly invest 5-10 hours per interview. Spread it across several days: research and question prediction first, STAR answer building next, and spoken practice in the final day or two.

What is the STAR method?

A structure for competency answers: Situation, Task, Action, Result. UK panels in the NHS, Civil Service and most large employers are trained to score answers in this shape, with most marks attached to your specific actions and the outcome.

How many questions should I prepare answers for?

Prepare 6-8 strong, flexible STAR examples rather than scripting dozens of answers. Well-chosen examples can each answer several different competency questions.

What should I do the day before an interview?

Confirm logistics or test your video setup, re-read the job advert and your examples, prepare your questions for the panel, and stop preparing by early evening - rest beats last-minute cramming.
