The UK labour market in 2026

Competition for roles has intensified sharply since the post-pandemic hiring peak. According to the Office for National Statistics, UK vacancies fell to around 707,000–711,000 in spring 2026 — the lowest level since early 2021 and roughly half the mid-2022 peak of about 1.3 million. The ONS also reports approximately 2.5 unemployed people per vacancy in early 2026, up from under 2 a year earlier.

  • ~711,000 UK job vacancies (Jan–Mar 2026) — lowest since early 2021 (ONS)
  • 2.5 unemployed people per vacancy in early 2026 (ONS)
  • 4.9% UK unemployment rate, Dec 2025–Feb 2026 (ONS)

How many people apply for each job?

Application volumes have surged as tools make applying easier. Recruitment platform Tribepad recorded an average of 48.7 applications per advertised job in late 2024 — a near-tripling year on year — while 2026 industry reporting suggests some professional roles now attract 250–300 applications, with popular graduate schemes exceeding that. Research cited by StandOut CV suggests only around 2% of applicants to a typical role are invited to interview, and recruiters spend roughly 30 seconds on an initial CV read.

The interview process itself

Once shortlisted, candidates face longer, more structured processes than before 2022. Industry analyses put the average UK time-to-hire for professional roles at around 6–8 weeks, with public sector and regulated roles often longer. Multi-stage processes are now standard, and the majority of employers use video interviews at early stages. UK candidates commonly report investing 5–10 hours preparing for a single interview.

What employers say they look for

Employer surveys consistently point the same way: structured evidence of behaviour beats claims. Around 70% of employers say they prioritise soft skills and behavioural evidence over formal qualifications at interview — which is why competency and values-based formats (and the STAR structure for answering them) dominate UK hiring, from the NHS and Civil Service to graduate schemes.

The cost of interviewing

Interviews cost candidates real money as well as time. UK estimates put the average cost of attending an interview at roughly £40–£55 (travel, time off, clothing), rising towards £100 for younger candidates. Multiplied across a search that industry data suggests averages several months, preparation efficiency matters.

What this means for your preparation

The pattern across every source is consistent: fewer vacancies, far more applicants per role, and interviews that reward structured, evidence-based answers. The candidates who convert interviews into offers are the ones who prepare for the specific competencies a panel will assess — which is exactly what our free sector guides and the Interview Coach UK app are built for.

Key takeaways

  • Vacancies are at their lowest since 2021; competition per role has roughly doubled in two years
  • Only a small fraction of applicants reach interview — the interview itself is the highest-leverage stage
  • UK employers overwhelmingly assess behaviour and evidence, not just qualifications
  • Structured STAR preparation targets exactly what panels score

Frequently asked questions

How many people apply for each job in the UK?

Industry data ranges from an average of roughly 49 applications per advertised job (Tribepad, late 2024) to 250-300 for some professional and graduate roles in 2026 reporting. Volumes vary widely by sector and seniority.

What percentage of job applicants get an interview in the UK?

Research cited in UK recruitment roundups suggests only around 2% of applicants to a typical role are invited to interview, which is why the interview stage is so decisive once reached.

How long does the UK hiring process take in 2026?

Around 6-8 weeks on average for professional roles according to 2026 industry analyses, with public sector and regulated roles often taking longer.

How much does attending an interview cost?

UK estimates put the average at roughly forty to fifty-five pounds per interview in travel, time and clothing, rising towards one hundred pounds for candidates aged 16-24.
